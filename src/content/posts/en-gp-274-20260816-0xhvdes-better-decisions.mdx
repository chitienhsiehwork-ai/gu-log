# The Principles of Better Decisions

Every day you make hundreds of decisions. Some are small. What book to read. Who to follow. Which podcast to listen to.
Others completely change the direction of your life. Which career to pursue. Who to work with. What company to build. Where to invest your time.
Most people think better decisions come from having more information.
They don't.
They come from having a better way of thinking. That's why some people can look at the same situation and make completely different decisions.
One sees risk. Another sees opportunity.
One sees a dead end. Another builds a billion-dollar company. The difference usually isn't intelligence. It's the mental models they use to think.

<MoguNote summary="Use the seven models together, not as isolated flash cards.">
These seven models work better together than as separate things to memorize. The conference example below counts only the second-order upside of possibly meeting the right person. Put opportunity cost and probability back into the picture, and the real question becomes: are the ticket price and time worth that low-probability upside? This piece isn't deep, but it works well as a map. Start with the [glossary](/en/glossary), dig into the older posts, then run one real decision from today through all seven models. That will do more than memorizing seven labels. （・∀・）
</MoguNote>

# **1: [First Principles Thinking](/en/glossary#first-principles-thinking)**

One of the biggest mistakes people make is starting with, "How does everyone else do this?" Instead, start with a different question.
"What is fundamentally true?"
That's exactly how first principles thinking works.
Instead of copying existing solutions, you break a problem down into its most basic truths and build upward from there.
**For example:**
Many founders think they need investors before they can build a startup.
**But do they?** Not necessarily. Maybe what they actually need is to find their first paying customers.
Many people think they need an office to build a company.
**But do they?** Or do they need a laptop and an internet connection?

The moment you stop copying assumptions and start questioning them, you begin finding solutions most people never even consider. Instead of inheriting other people's assumptions, you create your own.

# **2: [Opportunity Cost](/en/glossary#opportunity-cost)**

Every decision has a hidden price because of what it replaces.

1. **Watching Netflix** isn't just watching Netflix. It's choosing that over learning a new skill.
1. **Buying a new phone** isn't just buying a phone. It's choosing not to invest that money somewhere else.
1. **Scrolling for three hours** isn't just entertainment. It's three hours you didn't spend writing, building, learning, or meeting someone new.

Every "yes" automatically becomes a "no" to something else. The moment you start thinking in trade-offs instead of purchases, your decisions become much clearer.

Opportunity cost isn't about feeling guilty every time you relax. It's about becoming intentional. Because the life you build is simply the result of what you repeatedly choose over everything else.

# **3: [Second-Order Thinking](/en/glossary#second-order-thinking)**

Most people make decisions based on the first outcome they can see. The best decision-makers think one or two steps further.
**For example:**
Skipping a conference saves you $500. That's the first-order effect.
But what happens next?

- You don't meet the founder who later becomes your biggest client.
- Or the investor who introduces you to your future co-founder.
- Or the engineer who later becomes your co-founder.

The same applies everywhere. Publishing one article probably won't change your life. But one article leads to one new connection. That connection leads to a new opportunity. That opportunity changes the trajectory of your career.
Most life-changing decisions don't look life-changing when you make them. They only become obvious looking backward.
That's why the best decisions aren't always the ones with the biggest immediate reward. They're usually the ones that create the most opportunities later.

# **4: [Compounding](/en/glossary#compounding)**

One workout won't change your body. One article won't build your personal brand. One conversation won't build your network. One book won't change your life. That's why most people quit too early.
They're looking for immediate results from things that only become valuable after hundreds of repetitions.
The opposite is also true. One day of procrastination doesn't matter. Neither does one hour of doomscrolling.
But repeated often enough, small decisions quietly become your future. Compounding works whether you're building good habits or bad ones.

The only question is: what are your daily decisions compounding into?
Nobody becomes exceptional because of one extraordinary day. They become exceptional because ordinary days were repeated for years.

# **5: [Incentives](/en/glossary#incentives)**

If you want to understand why people do what they do, stop listening only to what they say.
Look at what benefits them.

- An influencer recommends a product. Why?
- A founder says every startup should raise VC funding. Why?
- A company tells you working from the office is better. Why?
- A YouTuber says you should quit your job tomorrow. Why?

Every opinion comes with incentives.
That doesn't mean people are lying. It means every recommendation is shaped by someone's goals, business model, or worldview.
The moment you start asking, "**What does this person gain if I believe them?**" you begin filtering information much more effectively.
You stop blindly copying advice.
You start understanding the incentives behind it.

# **6: [Probabilistic Thinking](/en/glossary#probabilistic-thinking)**

One of the biggest mistakes people make is waiting until they're 100% certain. That moment almost never comes.

- Investors don't know if a company will become the next unicorn.
- Founders don't know if their startup will work.
- Creators don't know if a post will go viral.

They make decisions anyway. Life isn't about certainty. It's about increasing your odds.
Instead of asking, "Will this work?" ask, "Does this increase my probability of succeeding?"

- Starting a personal brand probably won't guarantee success. But it increases your chances.
- Going to a conference probably won't change your life. But it increases your chances.
- Talking to one more founder probably won't lead to your next company. But it increases your chances.

The people who win aren't the ones who predict the future. They're the ones who consistently place better bets.

# **7: [Inversion Thinking](/en/glossary#inversion-thinking)**

The best decision-makers don't always think forward. Sometimes they think backward. Before asking what leads to success, they identify the behaviors that almost **guarantee failure**:

- Never publish.
- Never talk to users.
- Never take risks.
- Never ask questions.
- Never start.

Avoid enough of those, and you're already ahead of most people.

## **Now here's what I actually want you to take away from all of this.**

Every decision you make, even the smallest one, shapes the trajectory of your career in the long run. That's why it's so important to start thinking this way as early as possible. Learn how to think, and the decisions will follow. And eventually, so will the results. ❤️
